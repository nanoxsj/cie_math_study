1.6 Series

Binomial expansion · arithmetic progressions · geometric progressions · sums

CIE 9709 Paper 1 Pure Mathematics 1, syllabus 1.6.
重要本节考纲要求

本页依据 9709 Mathematics 2026–2027 syllabus 1.6 Series：

  • 使用 (a+b)^n 的 expansion；
  • 识别 arithmetic progression 与 geometric progression；
  • 使用 nth-term 与 first-n-terms sum formula；
  • 使用 geometric progression convergence condition 和 sum to infinity；
  • 判断 numbers 是否构成 AP 或 GP，并解决含有多个 progression 的问题。

下列 19 组题目均来自工作区保存的 Paper 1 原始 past-paper PDF。题干只把 PDF 排版符号整理为 LaTeX，没有改写或编造题目；答案按对应 mark scheme 的得分步骤展开。

1. Core knowledge

Binomial expansion

(a+b)^n=a^n+{}^nC_1a^{n-1}b+{}^nC_2a^{n-2}b^2+\cdots+b^n.

找 x^r 的 coefficient 时，只保留会产生该幂次的 terms；乘积 expansion 则把各项的幂次相加后收集同次幂。

Arithmetic progression

第一项为 a、common difference 为 d 的 AP：

u_n=a+(n-1)d,\qquad S_n=\frac n2\bigl(2a+(n-1)d\bigr).

三个连续 numbers 为 AP 的等价条件是 2b=a+c。已知两个 sums 时，分别写出 S_m 与 S_n，再联立求 a,d。

Geometric progression

第一项为 a、common ratio 为 r 的 GP：

u_n=ar^{n-1},\qquad S_n=a\frac{1-r^n}{1-r}\quad(r\ne1).

当 |r|<1 时收敛，并且

S_\infty=\frac a{1-r}.

三个连续 numbers 为 GP 的等价条件是 b^2=ac。处理题目时保留正负 root，最后根据 positive terms、convergence 或题目条件筛选。

Binomial general term and coefficients

当 n 是 non-negative integer 时，binomial expansion 的 general term 是

T_{r+1}=\binom nr a^{n-r}b^r,qquad r=0,1,\ldots,n.

求指定项或 coefficient 时先确定 r，再把各因素的 powers 相乘；若多个 expansion 相乘，要收集总次数相同的 terms。含有小参数 x 的题目要按题目要求保留到指定 power，不能把无关高次项混入。

AP, GP and recurrence checks

AP 的相邻 terms 差相等：u_{n+1}-u_n=d；GP 的相邻 terms 比相等：u_{n+1}/u_n=r。也可以使用 recurrence form

u_{n+1}=u_n+d\quad\text{或}\quad u_{n+1}=ru_n.

有限 GP 的 sum 也可写为

S_n=\frac{a(r^n-1)}{r-1},\qquad r\ne1.

只有 |r|<1 时 S_\infty 存在；若 r 为 negative，terms 会交替变号，但 convergence condition 仍是 |r|<1。涉及两个 AP/GP 的题目应分别写 nth term 或 sum equation，再联立求未知量。

Question 1 · 9709/11/M/J/20 Q1

The sum of the first nine terms of an arithmetic progression is 117. The sum of the next four terms is 91. Find the first term and the common difference. [4]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

Let the first term be a and the common difference be d.

S_9=9a+36d=117\Longrightarrow a+4d=13.

The next four terms are terms 10 to 13:

4a+42d=91.

Multiplying the first simplified equation by 4 and subtracting gives 26d=39, so d=3/2. Hence

\boxed{a=7,\qquad d=\frac32}.
Source: PastPaper/2020/2020/9709-s20-qp-11.pdf, Q1, and matching 9709-s20-ms-11.pdf.

Question 2 · 9709/12/M/J/20 Q4

The nth term of an arithmetic progression is \frac12(3n-15). Find the value of n for which the sum of the first n terms is 84. [5]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

The first term is u_1=-6 and the common difference is d=3/2. Therefore

S_n=\frac n2\left(2(-6)+(n-1)\frac32\right)=84.

Multiplying by 4:

3n^2-27n-336=0 \Longrightarrow n^2-9n-112=0 \Longrightarrow(n-16)(n+7)=0.

Since n is positive,

\boxed{n=16}.
Source: PastPaper/2020/2020/9709-s20-qp-12.pdf, Q4, and matching 9709-s20-ms-12.pdf.

Question 3 · 9709/13/M/J/20 Q8

The first term of a progression is \sin^2\theta and the second term is \sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta, where 0<\theta<\frac12\pi. Given that the progression is geometric, find the sum to infinity. [3]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

The first term and ratio are

a=\sin^2\theta,\qquad r=\frac{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta}=\cos^2\theta.

Because 0<\theta<\pi/2, 0<r<1. Therefore

S_\infty=\frac{\sin^2\theta}{1-\cos^2\theta} =\boxed{1}.
Source: PastPaper/2020/2020/9709-s20-qp-13.pdf, Q8, and matching 9709-s20-ms-13.pdf.

Question 4 · 9709/11/M/J/21 Q2

The sum of the first 20 terms of an arithmetic progression is 405 and the sum of the first 40 terms is 1410. Find the 60th term. [5]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

S_{20}=10(2a+19d)=405\Longrightarrow2a+19d=40.5,

S_{40}=20(2a+39d)=1410\Longrightarrow2a+39d=70.5.

Subtracting gives 20d=30, so d=1.5. Then 2a+28.5=40.5, hence a=6. Therefore

\boxed{u_{60}=a+59d=6+59(1.5)=94.5}.
Source: PastPaper/2021/2021/9709_s21_qp_11.pdf, Q2, and matching 9709_s21_ms_11.pdf.

Question 5 · 9709/11/M/J/21 Q5

The fifth, sixth and seventh terms of a geometric progression are 8k, -12 and 2k respectively. Given that k is negative, find the sum to infinity. [4]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

For consecutive GP terms,

(-12)^2=(8k)(2k)\Longrightarrow144=16k^2.

Thus k=\pm3, and the condition k<0 gives k=-3. The terms are -24,-12,-6,\ldots, so r=1/2. The first term is

a=\frac{-24}{(1/2)^4}=-384.

Therefore

\boxed{S_\infty=\frac{-384}{1-\frac12}=-768}.
Source: PastPaper/2021/2021/9709_s21_qp_11.pdf, Q5, and matching 9709_s21_ms_11.pdf.

Question 6 · 9709/12/M/J/21 Q8(a–b)

The first three terms of an AP are a,\frac32a,b. The first three terms of a GP are a,18,b+3. Find a,b and the sum of the first 20 terms of the AP.

Show detailed mark scheme answer

For the AP,

b=2\left(\frac32a\right)-a=2a.

For the GP,

18^2=a(b+3)=a(2a+3).

Thus 2a^2+3a-324=0, giving a=12 or a=-27/2. Positivity selects a=12, so b=24. The AP difference is d=18-12=6. Therefore

\boxed{S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}\bigl(2(12)+19(6)\bigr)=1380}.
Source: PastPaper/2021/2021/9709_s21_qp_12.pdf, Q8(a–b), and matching 9709_s21_ms_12.pdf.

Question 7 · 9709/13/M/J/21 Q9(a)

A geometric progression has second term equal to 24\% of its sum to infinity. Find the possible values of the common ratio. [3]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

Let the first term be a and common ratio be r. Then

ar=0.24\frac a{1-r}.

Cancel a and rearrange:

r(1-r)=0.24\Longrightarrow r^2-r+0.24=0.

Thus (r-0.4)(r-0.6)=0, so

\boxed{r=\frac25\text{ or }\frac35}.
Source: PastPaper/2021/2021/9709_s21_qp_13.pdf, Q9(a), and matching 9709_s21_ms_13.pdf.

Question 8 · 9709/12/F/M/21 Q9(a–b)

The first term of a progression is \cos\theta, where 0<\theta<\frac12\pi. For the GP case, the sum to infinity is 1/\cos\theta. (i) Show that the second term is \cos\theta\sin^2\theta. (ii) Find the sum of the first 12 terms when \theta=\frac13\pi, correct to 4 significant figures.

Show detailed mark scheme answer

For a GP,

\frac{\cos\theta}{1-r}=\frac1{\cos\theta} \Longrightarrow1-r=\cos^2\theta \Longrightarrow r=\sin^2\theta.

Hence the second term is

\boxed{ar=\cos\theta\sin^2\theta}.

When \theta=\pi/3, a=1/2 and r=3/4. Therefore

S_{12}=\frac12\frac{1-(3/4)^{12}}{1-3/4} =2\left(1-\left(\frac34\right)^{12}\right) =\boxed{1.937\text{ (4 s.f.)}}.
Source: PastPaper/2021/2021/9709_m21_qp_12.pdf, Q9(a)(i–ii), and matching 9709_m21_ms_12.pdf.

Question 9 · 9709/12/F/M/22 Q4

The first term of a GP and the first term of an AP are both a. The third GP term equals the second AP term, and the fifth GP term equals the sixth AP term. All terms are positive and not all equal. Find the sum of the first 20 AP terms in terms of a. [6]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

Let the GP ratio be r and the AP difference be d. Then

ar^2=a+d,\qquad ar^4=a+5d.

From the first equation, d=a(r^2-1). Substitution into the second gives

ar^4=a+5a(r^2-1)\Longrightarrow r^4-5r^2+4=0.

Thus r^2=1 or 4. Not all terms equal rejects r^2=1; positivity selects r=2. Hence d=3a, and

\boxed{S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}\bigl(2a+19(3a)\bigr)=590a}.
Source: PastPaper/2022/2022/9709_m22_qp_12.pdf, Q4, and matching 9709_m22_ms_12.pdf.

Question 10 · 9709/12/F/M/23 Q4(a–b)

The circumference of a tree is 5.00 m. After one year it is 5.02 m. (a) Given that the circumferences at yearly intervals form an AP, find the circumference 20 years after the first measurement. (b) Given instead that they form a GP, find the circumference 20 years after the first measurement.

Show detailed mark scheme answer
  1. The AP has a=5.00 and d=0.02. Twenty years after the first measurement is the 21st term:

\boxed{u_{21}=5+20(0.02)=5.40\text{ m}}.

  1. The GP ratio is r=5.02/5=1.004. Therefore
\boxed{u_{21}=5(1.004)^{20}=5.42\text{ m (3 s.f.)}}.

Question 11 · 9709/11/M/J/24 Q8(a–b)

  1. The first three AP terms are 25, 4p-1, 13-p. Find the tenth term. (b) The first three GP terms are 25, 4q-1, 13-q, where q is positive. Find the sum to infinity.
Show detailed mark scheme answer
  1. AP condition:

2(4p-1)=25+(13-p)\Longrightarrow9p=40.

Thus p=40/9, d=(4p-1)-25=-74/9, and

\boxed{u_{10}=25+9\left(-\frac{74}{9}\right)=-49}.

  1. GP condition:

(4q-1)^2=25(13-q) \Longrightarrow16q^2+17q-324=0.

The roots are q=4 and q=-81/16; positivity selects q=4. The GP is 25,15,9,\ldots, so r=3/5:

\boxed{S_\infty=\frac{25}{1-\frac35}=\frac{125}{2}}.
Source: PastPaper/2024/2024/qp-202406-mathematics-p11.pdf, Q8(a–b), and matching ms-202406-mathematics-p11.pdf.

Question 12 · 9709/12/M/J/24 Q5(a–b)

The first and second AP terms are \tan\theta and \sin\theta, where 0<\theta<\frac12\pi. (a) Given \theta=\frac14\pi, find the exact sum of the first 40 terms. (b) Find the sum to infinity of the GP with the same first two terms, in terms of \theta.

Show detailed mark scheme answer

For \theta=\pi/4, the first term is a=1 and the second is \sqrt2/2, so

d=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}-1.

Therefore

\boxed{S_{40}=20\left(2+39\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}-1\right)\right)=10(39\sqrt2-35)}.

For the GP, a=\tan\theta and r=\sin\theta/\tan\theta=\cos\theta. Hence

\boxed{S_\infty=\frac{\tan\theta}{1-\cos\theta}}.

Question 13 · 9709/13/M/J/24 Q7(a–b)

The first term of an AP is 1.5 and the sum of the first ten terms is 127.5. (a) Find the common difference. (b) Find the sum of all terms whose values are between 25 and 100.

Show detailed mark scheme answer

127.5=\frac{10}{2}\bigl(2(1.5)+9d\bigr) \Longrightarrow25.5=3+9d \Longrightarrow\boxed{d=2.5}.

The nth term is u_n=1.5+2.5(n-1)=2.5n-1. The terms strictly between 25 and 100 are u_{11}=26.5 through u_{40}=99. Thus

\boxed{S=\frac{30}{2}(26.5+99)=1882.5}.

Question 14 · 9709/13/M/J/24 Q10(a–c)

The GP a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots has first term 2 and common ratio r, where r\ne0. It is given that \frac92a_5+7a_3=8. (a) Find r. (b) Find the sum of the first 20 terms. (c) Find the sum to infinity of a_2,a_5,a_8,\ldots.

Show detailed mark scheme answer

Since a_3=2r^2 and a_5=2r^4,

\frac92(2r^4)+7(2r^2)=8\Longrightarrow9r^4+14r^2-8=0.

Factorising:

(9r^2-4)(r^2+1)=0.

The real positive ratio is r=2/3. Hence

\boxed{S_{20}=2\frac{1-(2/3)^{20}}{1-2/3}=5.998\text{ (4 s.f.)}}.

The sequence a_2,a_5,a_8,\ldots has first term a_2=4/3 and ratio r^3=8/27, so

\boxed{S_\infty=\frac{4/3}{1-8/27}=\frac{36}{19}}.
Source: PastPaper/2024/2024/qp-202406-mathematics-p13.pdf, Q10(a–c), and matching ms-202406-mathematics-p13.pdf.

Question 15 · 9709/11/O/N/24 Q10(a–b)

An AP has first term 5 and common difference d>0. The second, fifth and eleventh AP terms are the first three terms of a GP. (a) Find d. (b) If S_{77} is the AP sum and G_{10} is the sum of the first 10 GP terms, find S_{77}-G_{10}.

Show detailed mark scheme answer

The three GP terms are 5+d, 5+4d, 5+10d. Hence

(5+4d)^2=(5+d)(5+10d).

Expanding gives 6d^2-15d=0. Since d>0, d=5/2. The GP is 15/2,15,30,\ldots, with ratio 2. Therefore

S_{77}=\frac{77}{2}\left(10+76\cdot\frac52\right)=7700,

G_{10}=\frac{15}{2}(2^{10}-1)=7672.5.

Thus

\boxed{S_{77}-G_{10}=27.5}.
Source: PastPaper/2024/2024/qp-202410-mathematics-p11.pdf, Q10(a–b), and matching ms-202410-mathematics-p11.pdf.

Question 16 · 9709/12/O/N/24 Q2(a–b)

The first term of an AP is -20 and the common difference is 5. Find the sum of the first 20 terms. [2]

  1. It is given that the sum of the first 2k terms is 10 times the sum of the first k terms. Find the value of k. [3]
Show detailed mark scheme answer

S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}\bigl(2(-20)+19(5)\bigr) =10(55) =\boxed{550}.

  1. For this AP,

S_n=\frac n2\left[-40+5(n-1)\right]=\frac52n(n-9).

Hence

\frac52(2k)(2k-9)=10\cdot\frac52k(k-9).

Since k>0, cancellation gives 2k-9=5k-45, so

\boxed{k=12}.

Question 17 · 9709/13/O/N/24 Q1

An AP has fourth term 15 and eighth term 25. Find the 30th term. [3]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

a+3d=15,\qquad a+7d=25.

Subtracting gives 4d=10, so d=5/2 and a=15-3(5/2)=15/2. Hence

\boxed{u_{30}=\frac{15}{2}+29\left(\frac52\right)=80}.

Question 18 · 9709/13/O/N/24 Q6

The first term of a convergent GP is 10. The sum of the first four terms is p and the sum of the first eight terms is q. It is given that q=\frac{17}{16}p. Find the two possible values of the sum to infinity. [5]

Show detailed mark scheme answer

Using S_n=10(1-r^n)/(1-r),

\frac{S_8}{S_4}=\frac{1-r^8}{1-r^4}=1+r^4=\frac{17}{16}.

Thus r^4=1/16, or equivalently

16r^8-17r^4+1=0=(16r^4-1)(r^4-1).

The convergent real ratios are r=\frac12 and r=-\frac12. Therefore

\boxed{S_\infty=20\text{ or }\frac{20}{3}}.
Source: PastPaper/2024/2024/qp-202410-mathematics-p13.pdf, Q6, and matching ms-202410-mathematics-p13.pdf.

Question 19 · 9709/11/M/J/21 Q3(a–b)

  1. Find the first three terms in ascending powers of x of (3-2x)^5. (b) Hence find the coefficient of x^2 in (4+x)^2(3-2x)^5.
Show detailed mark scheme answer

Using the binomial theorem,

(3-2x)^5=3^5+5(3^4)(-2x)+10(3^3)(-2x)^2+\cdots =243-810x+1080x^2+\cdots.

Since (4+x)^2=16+8x+x^2, the coefficient of x^2 is

16(1080)+8(-810)+243=\boxed{11043}.
Source: PastPaper/2021/2021/9709_s21_qp_11.pdf, Q3(a–b), and matching 9709_s21_ms_11.pdf.
